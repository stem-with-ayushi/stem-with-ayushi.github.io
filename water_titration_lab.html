🧮 Step-by-Step Stoichiometric Acidity Determination

Criterion C: Mathematical processing of aqueous neutralization
Standard 1:1 Net Neutralization: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)
STEP 1

Calculate Moles of NaOH Used

n(NaOH) = C(NaOH) × V̄_NaOH (L)

Using standardized base concentration C = 0.100 mol·L⁻¹ and our mean concordant titer volume in liters:

n(NaOH) = -- mol
STEP 2

Stoichiometric Acid Equivalence

n(H⁺ neutralized) = n(OH⁻ added)

Because hydrogen ions and hydroxide ions react in an exact 1:1 molar ratio at the neutral equivalence point:

n(H⁺) = -- mol
STEP 3

Total Titratable Acidity of Water Sample

[H⁺] = n(H⁺) ÷ V_water aliquot (L)

Concentration of neutralizable acidic protons in the environmental water body:

[H⁺] = -- mol·L⁻¹
STEP 4

Standard Environmental Metric (CaCO₃ Eq.)

Acidity (mg/L CaCO₃) = ([H⁺] ÷ 2) × 100,090

Environmental regulatory agencies (EPA / WHO) quantify environmental acidity as equivalent calcium carbonate needed for neutralization:

Acidity = -- mg/L CaCO₃

🏞️ Comparative Acidity Across 5 Aquatic Ecosystems

Empirical titratable acidity and pH thresholds
Quarry / Acid Mine Drainage pH 3.10 · 22.4 mL NaOH
Severe sulfuric acid leaching. Heavy metals (Al³⁺, Fe³⁺) highly mobile. Fish reproductive failure.
Industrial River (Downstream) pH 4.25 · 14.8 mL NaOH
Acid precipitation runoff (SO₂/NOₓ). Loss of sensitive insects (mayflies, stoneflies).
Agricultural Runoff Canal pH 4.95 · 9.6 mL NaOH
Ammonium nitrification and phosphate fertilizer run-off. Sub-lethal stress to amphibians.
Woodland Peat Pond pH 5.65 · 4.2 mL NaOH
Naturally buffered by humic and fulvic organic tannins. Adapted macroinvertebrate community.
Pristine Alpine Stream (Control) pH 6.75 · 0.6 mL NaOH
Healthy balanced watershed. High dissolved oxygen, abundant trout and diverse biodiversity.

🚜 Lake Liming & Watershed Remediation Model

Calculating agricultural lime (CaCO₃) required to restore ecosystem pH

When lakes succumb to critical acidification (pH < 5.0), ecological intervention via Liming is deployed in Sweden, Norway, and North America. Powered limestone (CaCO₃) or slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂) is dispersed by helicopters and boats.

Remediation Reaction:
CaCO₃(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Ca²⁺(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)
